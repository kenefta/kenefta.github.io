Try on Another Writer's Style

In this exercise, you will rewrite a professional writer's sample using your own words. The goal is to practice "try on" a professional writer's sentence structure and punctuation. This exercise is designed to help you become more aware of how different writers construct their sentences and paragraphs for stylistic effect so you can incorporate these techniques into your own writing.

To get started, select a writing sample from the dropdown menu. Then, rewrite the sample in your own words, using the same word count and punctionation as the original writer. Click the Tab key to quick move to the next word input box.

Once you've completed your rewrite, click Submit to contrast your own writing with the orignal.


A hallmark of fluid, effective writing is syntactic complexity: a writer's use of modifying clauses, punctionation for meaning and effect, varying length of sentences and paragraphs, etc. When I taught English 101 while in Grad School, I used a paper version of this exercise to encourage my brand-new college students "try on" the syntactic styles of professional writers. My teaching goal was turn my high-school writers into college-level writers (they were writing very declarative senences, five sentences in each paragraph, and five paragraphes per essay). My goal for this Web app experiement was to recreate the paper exercise as an online app using only AI code-gen.

Feel free to try out this exercise yourself!

This app was generated by the AI code-gen tool Vondy, using only prompts, in about 20-30 minutes. I've fine tuned the app using VS Code.
